# MEGA Customer Value Challenge – Final Report

Final report of **Team 25 (Yellow, Universe 5)** for the *Strategy and Organization* course. The report reflects on our decisions and results in **The Customer Value Challenge**, a business simulation by MEGA Learning in which teams run a car manufacturing company from a General Management perspective.

## About the simulation

Each team manages a company with four product lines (Low Cost, Family, Eco-Friendly and Image) and takes decisions every period on pricing, marketing, engineering, production volume, facility investment, HR & Quality, dividends and loans. Decisions are made under liquidity constraints and in a changing economic environment, against competing teams.

## Our strategy

We adopted a segment-specific positioning instead of a uniform approach:

- **Low Cost**: cost leadership, standardization and competitive pricing
- **Family**: preserving market share through comfort, robustness, size and fuel efficiency
- **Eco-Friendly**: a long-term growth bet, supported by early investments in engineering and capacity
- **Image**: selective investment, as it required continuous spending on appeal and marketing

In Region 2 we chose a selective entry, focusing on Low Cost and Family and serving Eco-Friendly through exports.

## Key lessons

1. **Being competitive in every segment is not a strategy.** Results improved when we focused on the segments where our resources and market conditions created a real fit, in line with the BCG growth matrix.
2. **Growth only creates value when it matches capacity.** Demand, production capacity and cash are connected: improving only one of them just moves the bottleneck.
3. **Liquidity is a strategic resource, not just a safety buffer.** Excessive caution about debt slowed our investments in scale-driven segments.

The report closes with a final reflection on the hardest trade-off (timing the entry into the second region) and on what we would change if we restarted the simulation.

## Frameworks and concepts used

BCG growth matrix, diversification vs. specialization, economies of scale and learning curve, capacity planning, financial leverage, first-mover advantage.
