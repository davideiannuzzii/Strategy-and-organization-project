# The Customer Value Challenge - Strategy & Organization

## Project Overview
This project documents the strategic analysis and decisions made during "The Customer Value Challenge" business simulation developed by MEGA Learning[cite: 1]. The objective of the simulation was to manage a car manufacturing company operating in four specific market segments: Low Cost, Family, Eco-Friendly, and Image[cite: 1]. The report analyzes the evolution of our corporate strategy through decisions regarding pricing, marketing, engineering, production volumes, and structural investments[cite: 1]. 

### 1. Strategic Focus vs. Diversification
* At the beginning of the simulation, we attempted to maintain a competitive presence across all segments with the goal of reducing risks[cite: 27].
* Over time, we realized that trying to be competitive everywhere reduced our ability to build a solid competitive advantage[cite: 28].
* We therefore concentrated our resources on the segments where we had the best fit, particularly Eco-Friendly and Low Cost[cite: 27, 28].
* The Family segment became unstable due to intense competition, proving that not all markets guarantee a defensible long-term advantage[cite: 27].
* The exit of two competitors from the Eco-Friendly segment in Region 1 provided us with the opportunity to consolidate a strong and profitable position[cite: 26].

### 2. Aligning Growth and Production Capacity
* We learned that growth only creates real value when it is supported by adequate production capacity and financial resources.
* In the Image segment, demand exceeded supply due to a capacity reduction (from 66 to 54 units), forcing us to increase prices (from 145 to 152) to realign sales with production[cite: 29].
* In the Low Cost segment, the strong capacity increase (reaching 225 units in Period 10) required strategic price reductions (from 41 to 26) to stimulate demand and avoid unused capacity[cite: 29].
* Expanding capacity without adequate demand generates excess inventory and unnecessary costs, as we experienced in the Family segment.

### 3. Liquidity as a Strategic Resource
* Initially, we managed liquidity with a defensive approach, avoiding debt to maintain a low risk profile[cite: 30].
* This overly conservative approach prevented us from timely expanding capacity in the Low Cost segment in Region 2, causing us to lose market share to competitors[cite: 31].
* In Period 8, taking a $5,000 loan allowed us to finance a capacity increase in the Eco-Friendly segment in Region 1 (growing from 120 to over 300 units by Period 10)[cite: 31].
* We concluded that debt is not just a financial risk, but a fundamental tool to fund growth opportunities and stay ahead of the competition[cite: 31].

## Conclusion & Reflections
The most complex strategic trade-off we faced was determining the right timing to enter Region 2[cite: 32]. We opted for a cautious approach based on exports rather than immediately opening local facilities[cite: 32]. This choice protected us from over-investing, but it probably caused us to miss out on the first-mover advantage[cite: 32]. In hindsight, a more aggressive use of financial leverage and greater protection of investments in employee morale would have further strengthened our long-term competitive position[cite: 32].
